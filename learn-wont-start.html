Car Won't Start: Battery, Alternator, or Starter?

A car that won't start is usually one of three things: the battery (stores power), the alternator (recharges the battery while you drive), or the starter (turns the engine over). What you hear when you turn the key is the best clue.

What's probably going on

Rapid clicking, dim lights

Get it checked soon

Classic weak or dead battery. A jump start usually gets you going.

Starts with a jump, then dies again in a day or two

Get it checked soon

The battery may be worn out, or the alternator isn't recharging it.

Battery warning light on while driving, lights dimming

Stop driving

Often the alternator. The car is running on battery power and may stall when it runs out.

One loud click, lights stay bright

Get it checked soon

Battery may be fine. Points toward the starter or its connection.

Engine cranks normally but won't fire up

Get it checked soon

Power is fine. The issue is more likely fuel, spark, or a sensor.

Nothing at all, no lights

Get it checked soon

Completely drained battery or a loose or corroded battery cable.

What you can safely check

  • Look at the battery terminals. White or green crusty buildup can block power.
  • Most batteries last about 3 to 5 years. Check for a date sticker on top.
  • If a jump works, drive at least 20 to 30 minutes to recharge, and don't shut it off somewhere you can't get help.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
