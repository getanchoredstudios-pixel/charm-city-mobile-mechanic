Engine Overheating: What to Do Right Now

Driving an overheating engine is one of the fastest ways to turn a cheap repair into a very expensive one. If the temperature gauge climbs into the red or you see steam, it's time to stop.

What's probably going on

Gauge in the red or steam from the hood

Stop driving

Pull over and shut it off. Continuing to drive can warp the engine and blow the head gasket.

Overheats in traffic but is fine on the highway

Get it checked soon

Often a cooling fan that isn't kicking on.

Sweet smell or green, orange, or pink puddles

Get it checked soon

Coolant leak, usually a hose, radiator, or water pump.

Heater blows cold air while the engine runs hot

Get it checked soon

Low coolant or a circulation problem.

Coolant keeps disappearing with no visible leak

Get it checked soon

Needs a closer look. Could be an internal leak.

What you can safely check

  • Never open the radiator cap on a hot engine. The coolant is under pressure and can cause serious burns. Wait at least 30 minutes.
  • Turning the heater on full blast pulls some heat away from the engine while you get to a safe spot.
  • Once fully cool, check the coolant reservoir (the plastic tank) against the MIN and MAX lines.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
