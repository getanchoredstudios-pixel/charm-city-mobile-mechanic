Car Shaking or Pulling? What It Usually Means

Vibration and pulling are easy to ignore until they get worse. The trick is noticing when it happens, since that points straight to the likely cause.

What's probably going on

Shakes at highway speeds (around 50 to 70 mph)

Get it checked soon

Most often tires out of balance or a damaged tire.

Shakes only when braking

Get it checked soon

Usually uneven brake rotors.

Shakes at a stop or at idle

Get it checked soon

Engine misfire or a worn motor mount.

Pulls to one side while driving

Get it checked soon

Low tire pressure on one side or the alignment is off. Baltimore potholes are famous for this.

Clunking over bumps

Get it checked soon

Worn suspension parts like links, ball joints, or struts.

Bulge on the side of a tire

Stop driving

Internal damage. The tire can fail suddenly.

What you can safely check

  • Check tire pressure on all four tires. The correct number is on a sticker inside the driver's door.
  • Look for bulges, bald spots, or wear only on the inside or outside edge of the tires.
  • Notice the speed and situation when it happens. That detail helps more than anything.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
