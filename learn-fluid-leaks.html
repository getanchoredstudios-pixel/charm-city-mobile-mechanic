What Color Is the Leak? A Fluid Guide

A puddle under the car can be harmless or a warning. Color, feel, and where it sits under the car tell you a lot. Here's a quick way to tell them apart.

What's probably going on

Clear and watery, near the front passenger side

Keep an eye on it

Usually condensation from the A/C. Normal, especially in summer.

Brown or black, slick

Get it checked soon

Engine oil. Check your dipstick. A small drip can wait a bit; a growing puddle can't.

Green, orange, or pink with a sweet smell

Get it checked soon

Coolant. Toxic to pets and kids, and it can lead to overheating.

Red or pinkish, oily

Get it checked soon

Usually transmission fluid, sometimes power steering fluid.

Blue, thin

Keep an eye on it

Windshield washer fluid. Not urgent.

Clear to light brown, slippery, near a wheel

Stop driving

Possibly brake fluid. Don't drive until it's checked.

What you can safely check

  • Slide a piece of white cardboard or paper under the car overnight. It shows the true color and exactly where the drip lands.
  • Touch a drop with a paper towel: is it thin like water or slick like oil?
  • Keep pets away from any green, orange, or pink puddles. Coolant tastes sweet and is poisonous.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
