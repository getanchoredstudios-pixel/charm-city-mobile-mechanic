Squealing, Grinding, or Shaking Brakes

Your brakes are designed to make noise before they fail. The sound they make tells you a lot about how much life is left.

What's probably going on

High-pitched squeal when braking

Get it checked soon

Most pads have a small metal tab that squeals when the pad is getting thin. It's a built-in reminder.

Grinding or scraping

Stop driving

The pad material is likely gone and metal is rubbing on metal. This eats the rotors fast and turns a pad job into a bigger bill.

Steering wheel or pedal shakes when stopping

Get it checked soon

Usually uneven rotors. Common after hard braking or long stretches without service.

Pedal feels soft or sinks to the floor

Stop driving

Possible fluid leak or air in the system. This one is a safety issue.

Car pulls to one side when braking

Get it checked soon

Could be a sticking caliper or uneven pad wear on one side.

What you can safely check

  • Look through the wheel spokes. If the pad looks thinner than about 1/8 inch (roughly 3 mm), it's due.
  • Check for wet spots on the inside of a tire. That can mean a brake fluid leak.
  • Notice whether the noise happens only when braking or all the time. That helps narrow it down.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
