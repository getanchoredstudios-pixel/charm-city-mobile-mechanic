Check Engine Light: Solid vs. Flashing

The check engine light means your car's computer saved a trouble code. It can be something as small as a loose gas cap or something that needs attention right away. How the light behaves is the biggest clue.

What's probably going on

Solid light, car drives normally

Get it checked soon

Something is out of range, but it's usually not an emergency. Get it scanned soon so a small problem stays small.

Flashing light

Stop driving

Usually a serious misfire. Unburned fuel can overheat and damage the catalytic converter, which is expensive to replace. Pull over when it's safe and limit driving.

Light plus shaking, loss of power, or strange smells

Stop driving

The car is telling you something is wrong now. Don't push it.

Light came on right after getting gas

Keep an eye on it

Often a loose or worn gas cap. Tighten it until it clicks. The light can take a few days of driving to turn off.

What you can safely check

  • Tighten the gas cap until it clicks.
  • Note whether the light is solid or flashing, and whether anything else on the dash is lit.
  • Pay attention to how the car feels: rough idle, hesitation, or worse gas mileage are all useful details.

This guide is general information to help you understand what's going on. It isn't a diagnosis. Curtis will confirm the cause and explain the price before any work begins.
